1. Why a Video Doorbell in 2026

The holidays are the busiest delivery season of the year, and the front porch is where most of it lands. A 2026 Security.org report estimated that 47.7 million Americans had a package stolen in the past year, which is why a doorbell camera has become the first smart-home purchase for many households rather than a luxury add-on. It answers the door when you are not home, records every visitor and delivery, and lets you tell a courier where to leave a parcel from anywhere.

Timing helps too. Black Friday falls on 27 November 2026, and the early deals are already live: the Ring Battery Doorbell 2K is at $39.99, its lowest price ever, and more doorbell bundles are announced for Black Friday week. If you were going to buy one this year, this is the window.

One honest note before the picks: the prices in this guide are deal-site reports checked on 9 November 2026, and they can change at any time. Always check the current price on Amazon before you buy. This guide makes no promise about sales or discounts beyond the prices we verified.

2. How We Evaluated

We compared the 2026 doorbell lineups from Ring, Blink, Arlo, and eufy on the factors that actually decide whether a doorbell is useful after the first week:

  • Video quality and field of view: 2K resolution is the useful baseline in 2026; a wide or tall view matters more than extra pixels when you need to see a package on the ground.
  • Power options: Battery models suit renters and homes without doorbell wiring; wired models never need charging but need an existing transformer and chime.
  • Subscription cost: Most brands charge a monthly plan to save recordings. We counted the plan as part of the real price, not an afterthought.
  • Ecosystem fit: Ring and Blink pair naturally with Alexa and Echo Show; eufy pairs with Alexa and Google Assistant.
  • Verified price: Every price below comes from a deal-site report or manufacturer listing checked in November 2026 — nothing is guessed.

4. How to Choose a Video Doorbell

The right doorbell depends on your front door, not on the spec sheet. Run through these five questions before you buy:

  • Do you have doorbell wiring? If yes, a wired model like the Blink Wired 2K+ or the Ring Pro 4K never needs charging. If no, or you rent, a battery model like the Ring 2K is the obvious choice — no electrician, no drilling into a transformer you do not own.
  • Do you want to see parcels on the ground? Most doorbells point straight ahead and miss the doorstep. The Arlo 2K's 180-degree view and the Ring 2K's 6x zoom are the two answers here: wide view or the ability to zoom into the package.
  • Will you pay a monthly fee? If the answer is no, buy the eufy E340. If yes, compare the plan, not just the doorbell: Blink Basic is $3.99 a month, Ring Solo is $4.99, and both are cheaper per year than per month.
  • Which voice assistant do you use? Ring and Blink are built for Alexa households — the feed shows on an Echo Show. eufy works with Alexa and Google Assistant. Match the doorbell to the speakers you already own.
  • Who needs to hear the ring? If your phone is often on silent, add a chime. The Ring bundle includes one for about $15 more than the doorbell alone at current prices.

One more practical check: measure your Wi-Fi signal at the front door before you buy. A doorbell on a weak signal will drop live view no matter how good its camera is. If the signal is poor, the Ring 2K Plus's dual-band Wi-Fi is worth considering, or move your router closer to the front of the house.

5. The Real Cost: Subscriptions

Doorbell makers rarely advertise the monthly plan on the box, so here it is plainly. Live View — watching the feed in real time — is free on Ring, and motion alerts are free across these brands. What the plans charge for is recording: saving clips, reviewing them later, and smart features like person detection.

  • Ring Solo: $4.99 a month or $49.99 a year for one device. Ring Multi, which covers every Ring camera in your home, is $99.99 a year.
  • Blink Basic: $3.99 a month or $39.99 a year. Blink Plus, for unlimited devices, is $119.99 a year.
  • Arlo Secure: A paid plan is needed after the trial for cloud recording and smart detections.
  • eufy E340: No plan at all. Recordings live on the doorbell's 8GB of built-in storage.

Over three years, a $39.99 Ring 2K plus Ring Solo costs about $190 in total; a $149.99 eufy E340 costs $149.99 in total and nothing more. The cheapest doorbell is not always the cheapest doorbell to own — do the three-year maths for your own situation before deciding.

The exceptions are clear. Buy the eufy E340 if you refuse a monthly fee — it is the only pick here with no subscription, and it pays for its premium within about two years.
