Best Budget Tablets Under $300 in 2026 — Top 5 Picks

A budget tablet should not feel like a compromise on the basics: a sharp screen, all-day battery, enough storage, and the apps you actually want. We compared the five strongest sub-$300 tablets of late 2026 — including Amazon's brand-new Alexa Tablets — so you can buy now or wait for Black Friday with confidence.

🔍 How We Evaluate Budget Tablets

We compare display resolution and refresh rate, battery capacity and rated hours, RAM and storage (plus microSD expansion), software and update support, and — the question that matters most at this price — whether the tablet runs full Android with Google Play or a locked-down app store. Prices below were checked on 25 October 2026; holiday prices move fast, so treat the "recent sale" figures as your Black Friday target, not a promise.

Quick Recommendations

Best overall: Samsung Galaxy Tab A11+ — the safest all-rounder, with a smooth 90Hz screen, a headphone jack, and security updates promised to 2032. Best screen for the money: Lenovo Idea Tab — a sharper 2.5K display that has been selling near $150. Best new small tablet: Amazon Alexa Tablet 8 — full Android and Google Play in a light, one-hand-friendly 8.7-inch body, though it is too new for a long-term verdict. Best ultra-cheap pick: Fire HD 10 (2023) — frequently under $75 on sale, but only if you are happy inside Amazon's app store. Best big-screen media tablet: Fire Max 11 — an 11-inch, 2000×1200 screen for streaming, now on clearance.

The One Question to Answer First: Fire OS or Full Android?

This is the single biggest source of disappointment in budget tablets, so let us settle it before the rankings. Amazon's older Fire tablets (Fire HD 10, Fire Max 11) run Fire OS. They have no Google Play Store and no official Google apps — you get Amazon's Appstore, Prime Video, Kindle and Alexa, but not the full Play catalog. Amazon's new Alexa Tablets, launched 8 October 2026, are different: they run full Android with Google Play, so the app problem goes away. Samsung and Lenovo tablets run full Android too.

Rule of thumb: if you want YouTube, Gmail, Google Drive, or a specific app your school or work uses, buy a full-Android tablet (Tab A11+, Idea Tab, or Alexa Tablet 8). If the tablet is mainly for Prime Video, Kindle reading, kids' content, or a second screen around the house, a Fire tablet on sale is excellent value — just buy it knowing its limits.

One more warning about clearance models: Amazon has confirmed it has stopped producing new Fire tablets, and the Fire line is being replaced by the Alexa Tablets. Fire HD 10 and Fire Max 11 remain supported, but buy them as clearance deals — not at full list price — because their replacements are already here.

#1 — Best Overall

Samsung Galaxy Tab A11+ Wi-Fi (128GB)

Est. ~$250 (recently $218–$230 on sale)

The Tab A11+ is the default answer for most buyers, because it gets the fundamentals right and keeps them right for years. You get an 11-inch 1920×1200 display at a smooth 90Hz, 6GB of RAM with 128GB of storage, a 7,040mAh battery with 25W charging, quad speakers with Dolby Atmos, a 3.5mm headphone jack, microSD expansion, and Android 16 with Samsung DeX. Samsung's update promise — security updates to 2032 — is the longest in this price class by a wide margin, and it is the real reason this tablet stays good value even at full price.

Watch the variant when you shop: the Wi-Fi 128GB model is the ~$250 one. The 5G version and the 256GB version cost more, and some store listings show different prices for those variants. If you see a price near $300, check whether you are looking at the 5G or 256GB model before assuming the Wi-Fi model has gone up.

Key Specifications: 11″ 1920×1200 LCD, 90Hz • 6GB RAM, 128GB storage, microSD • 7,040mAh, 25W charging • Quad speakers, Dolby Atmos • 3.5mm jack • 8MP rear, 5MP front • Android 16, DeX • Security updates to 2032

Pros
  • Best long-term support in this price class (updates to 2032)
  • Smooth 90Hz display and full Android with Google Play
  • Headphone jack, microSD, and big 7,040mAh battery
Cons
  • Display is LCD, not OLED — fine at this price, but blacks are grey
  • Sale prices vary by variant; check Wi-Fi 128GB specifically

#2 — Best Screen for the Money

Lenovo Idea Tab 11-inch (128GB)

Est. ~$240 list; $150 current sale

If screen sharpness is your priority, the Idea Tab wins this guide outright. Its 11-inch display runs at 2560×1600 — noticeably sharper than the 1920×1200 panels on the Samsung and Fire tablets — with a 90Hz refresh rate, and it pairs that with quad Dolby Atmos speakers. The 4GB/128GB configuration, MediaTek Dimensity 6300 chip, Android 15, microSD expansion, and stylus support make it a strong media and light-productivity tablet. At its current ~$150 sale price it is arguably the best value on this page; at its ~$240 list price the Samsung's longer update support is worth the small premium.

Do not confuse this model with Lenovo's bundles: an 8GB/128GB version with pen and folio case has sold for $259–$299, and the larger Idea Tab Plus (12.1-inch) lists at $339.99 and is over this guide's $300 cap. Match the listing to the 4GB/128GB 11-inch model before you compare prices.

Key Specifications: 11″ 2560×1600 (2.5K), 90Hz • MediaTek Dimensity 6300 • 4GB RAM, 128GB storage, microSD • Quad speakers, Dolby Atmos • Android 15 • Stylus support

Pros
  • Sharpest display in this guide (2.5K) at a budget price
  • Full Android with Google Play; stylus support
  • Outstanding value when on sale near $150
Cons
  • 4GB RAM is tighter for heavy multitasking than the Tab A11+'s 6GB
  • Update support is shorter than Samsung's

#3 — Best New Small Tablet

Amazon Alexa Tablet 8

Est. $229.99

The Alexa Tablet 8 is the most interesting new tablet in this guide, because it fixes the Fire line's biggest weakness. Announced 8 October 2026 and shipping in the US from 14 October, it runs full Android with Google Play — so every app that was missing on Fire tablets is here — alongside built-in Alexa+. The hardware is compact and genuinely portable: an 8.7-inch 1340×800 IPS display, a light aluminum body at 329g, 4GB of RAM, 64GB of storage (a 128GB configuration also exists), Wi-Fi 6, and a 4,600mAh battery rated up to 14 hours of video.

Two honest caveats. First, this tablet is weeks old, so there is no long-term reliability record yet — treat it as a strong early pick, not a proven one. Second, the fine print: the $229.99 base model shows lockscreen ads (the ad-free version is $249), and Alexa+ is free only with a Prime membership; without Prime it costs $19.99 per month. If you do not want Alexa+ at all, that subscription point does not matter — but do not buy this tablet expecting a free premium assistant without Prime.

Pros
  • Full Android with Google Play — no Fire OS app limits
  • Light, one-hand-friendly size with a premium-feeling aluminum body
  • Alexa+ built in for Prime members
Cons
  • Too new for a long-term verdict

#4 — Best Ultra-Cheap Pick

Amazon Fire HD 10 (2023)

Est. $154.99 list; $72.99 Prime sale

The Fire HD 10 (2023) is the cheapest way to get a genuinely usable 10-inch tablet — but only on sale, and only for the right buyer. Its 10.1-inch 1920×1200 display is sharp for the price, it has 3GB of RAM with 32GB of storage (a 64GB version exists), microSD expansion to 1TB, up to 13 hours of battery, USB-C, and a 3.5mm headphone jack. At its $72.99 Prime-member sale price in October 2026 — and a lowest-ever price of $69.99 — it is hard to beat for Prime Video, Kindle, and kids' content.

The catch is Fire OS: no Google Play Store, no official Google apps. If you need a specific Play app, this is the wrong tablet no matter how cheap it is. And because Amazon has ended Fire production in favor of the Alexa Tablets, treat the Fire HD 10 strictly as a clearance buy — never pay full list price for it in late 2026.

#5 — Best Big-Screen Media Tablet

Amazon Fire Max 11

Est. $229.99 list (clearance)

The Fire Max 11 is the Fire line's best media tablet: an 11-inch 2000×1200 display, 4GB of RAM, 64GB or 128GB of storage with microSD to 1TB, up to 14 hours of battery, Wi-Fi 6, and a fingerprint reader. For pure streaming and reading on a big screen, it is a step up from the Fire HD 10 in size, resolution, and speed.

The same clearance logic applies, though — and with a sharper edge. At $229.99 list, the Fire Max 11 now competes directly with the Alexa Tablet 8 (full Android, same price) and the Tab A11+ (full Android, longer support, similar price). So the Fire Max 11 only makes sense on a real sale — it has recently sold at $149.99 — or if you specifically want the Fire ecosystem. Note also that it has no headphone jack, unlike the Fire HD 10 and the Tab A11+, and its lockscreen ads cost $15 to remove.

Buy Now or Wait for Black Friday?

Black Friday 2026 falls on 27 November, with Cyber Monday on 30 November, and budget tablets are classic doorbuster items — so if you can wait five weeks, waiting is usually the right call. Use these October sale prices as your targets: Fire HD 10 around $70, Tab A11+ around $218–$230, Idea Tab near $150, and Fire Max 11 near $150. If a Black Friday price beats the target, buy it; if it merely matches the October price, you have lost nothing by waiting.

The one exception is the stretch pick below. Apple's iPad (A16) lists at $449 — it was raised from $349 in June 2026 — but it has been reported on sale at $299. That is not a sub-$300 tablet at list price, so it is not ranked here, but if Black Friday drops it to ~$299 again, it becomes the best tablet in this price conversation for anyone already in the Apple ecosystem. Wait for that drop; do not pay $449 and call it a budget buy.

Also worth knowing: the previous-generation Samsung Galaxy Tab A9+ is still floating around, but only buy it if it is clearly cheaper than the Tab A11+ — the A11+ is newer, better supported, and usually the same price or less. And the smaller Fire HD 8 (2024), around $100 at list and ~$80 on sale, is the cheapest entry point of all, but its 8-inch 1280×800 screen and 3GB of RAM make it a kids'-tablet or second-screen choice, not a main tablet.

Hidden Costs to Budget For

A tablet's sticker price is rarely the whole cost. A decent case runs $15–$30, a keyboard case more, and a stylus is an extra purchase on every tablet in this guide except where bundled. On Fire tablets, removing lockscreen ads costs $15. On the new Alexa Kids tablets, the included year of Amazon Kids+ converts to a paid subscription (from $7.99 per month with Prime) if you forget to cancel. And on the Alexa Tablet 8, remember that Alexa+ is $19.99 per month without Prime. None of these are deal-breakers — just add them to your mental total before you click buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which budget tablet is best for a child? The Fire HD 10 on sale, or the Alexa Tablet 8 if you want Google Play. Both are cheap enough to survive a drop, and Amazon's kids' content is strongest on its own tablets.

Can I use Google apps on a Fire tablet? Not officially. Fire tablets have no Google Play Store and no official Google apps. You can sideload, but we do not recommend it for a tablet you depend on — buy a full-Android tablet instead.

Is 64GB enough storage? For streaming and reading, yes — especially with microSD expansion on every tablet in this guide. For downloaded games, large offline video libraries, or school files, start at 128GB.

Should I buy the Fire Max 11 or wait for the Alexa Tablet 11? The Alexa Tablet 11 lists at $329.99, over this guide's cap. If your budget is firm at $300, the Fire Max 11 on clearance or the Tab A11+ are the better choices; if you can stretch, the Alexa line is the future of Amazon tablets.
