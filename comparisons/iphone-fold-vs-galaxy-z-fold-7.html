iPhone Duo (Apple Fold) vs Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7 (2026) — Flagship Foldable Showdown

Apple's first foldable is official: the iPhone Duo, announced September 9, 2026. We compare its 7.6-inch folding display, titanium precision hinge, iOS 27 multitasking, and dual 48MP cameras against Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 7.

1. Hinge Engineering & Inner Screen Crease

Samsung's dual-rail hinge folds the Z Fold 7 down to a slim closed profile, while Apple uses a precision hinge in a grade-5 titanium frame for the iPhone Duo. Apple's 7.6-inch inner display uses a 10-layer construction with a nano-texture finish designed to reduce glare and make the fold less visible, with an under-display FaceTime camera keeping the viewing surface uninterrupted.

Two details decide how a foldable feels after the novelty wears off: the hinge and the crease. Samsung is on its seventh generation of book-style foldables, and the Z Fold 7's dual-rail hinge is paired with an Advanced Armor aluminum frame — the phone is 4.2 mm thick when open, 8.9 mm when closed, and weighs 215 g, making it the lighter of these two despite having the larger inner screen. Apple's hinge is built from more than 100 components, with support ribs that hold the display flat when open and a magnet array that pulls the phone shut with a satisfying snap. The Duo is heavier at 254 g, and that 39 g gap is noticeable in a jacket pocket over a full day.

On the crease, both companies attack the same problem from different directions. Apple puts a nano-texture finish on the inner display to cut glare and make the fold less visible, and layers high-strength glass above and below the folding panel with adhesives that let the layers glide past each other as the phone bends. Samsung mounts its folding panel on a titanium support plate. Neither maker claims the crease is gone entirely, and it is most visible at an angle or under harsh overhead light — if you read or watch video mostly head-on, it fades from attention within days; if you work with the phone flat on a desk under office lighting, you will see it.

2. Multitasking Software: One UI vs. iOS 27

The true value of a book-style foldable lies in its tablet-sized interior workspace:

  • Samsung One UI: Offers the most mature power-user features: running three apps simultaneously in split-screen, drag-and-drop text/photos across windows, floating pop-up apps, and full desktop PC mode via Samsung DeX.
  • iPhone Duo (iOS 27): Brings native Split View, dual-window instances of the same app, and an expanded StandBy mode that works across both displays, with Apple Pencil (USB-C) support.

Samsung's advantage here is maturity. The Z Fold 7 launched with One UI 8 on Android 16, and Samsung promises seven years of OS and security updates — the longest support window in the foldable market, which matters enormously on a $1,999 purchase. Three-app split screen, drag-and-drop between windows, floating pop-up apps and Samsung DeX, which turns the phone into a desktop PC on an external display, are all features Samsung has refined across seven generations of foldables. Third-party Android apps have had years to adapt to the folding form factor, so the app library for the big screen is deeper.

Apple's answer is new but deliberately familiar. iOS 27 brings Split View to iPhone for the first time: two apps side by side, two windows of the same app (two Safari windows for comparison shopping, for example), saved app pairs you can return to, and a StandBy mode that works on either display even when the phone is not charging. Apple says Netflix, Zoom and Slack have already adapted their apps to the folding design. The stylus is a genuine advantage now: Apple Pencil (USB-C) support arrived with iOS 27.2 on 26 October 2026, so the Duo's headline stylus feature is one you can use from day one. If note-taking is the reason you want a foldable, make sure the Duo is updated to iOS 27.2 or later before you buy.

For raw multitasking breadth today, Samsung is ahead. For continuity — start a task on the cover display, finish it on the inner screen, hand it to your Mac — Apple's integration is the smoother experience, provided you already live in Apple's apps.

3. Camera Telephoto & Sensor Hardware

Foldable chassis constraints require thinner camera modules. The Galaxy Z Fold 7 leads on raw resolution with a 200MP main sensor plus a dedicated 3x telephoto, while the iPhone Duo uses a 48MP Dual Fusion system (Main + Ultra Wide) with a 12MP Center Stage front camera and an under-display FaceTime camera on the inner screen.

The spec-sheet gap is the telephoto. Samsung's 10MP 3x telephoto (67 mm) is a real optical zoom for portraits, events and travel, backed by a 200MP main sensor with optical image stabilization — the same class of sensor Samsung puts in its Galaxy S Ultra phones — and a 12MP ultra-wide. The Duo has no dedicated telephoto lens at all: its 48MP Fusion Main camera (f/1.6, sensor-shift stabilization) produces an optical-quality 2x zoom by cropping the sensor, and its 48MP Fusion Ultra Wide (f/2.2, 120-degree field of view) doubles as a macro camera. For anything beyond 2x, the Duo relies on digital zoom up to 10x, and that is where Samsung pulls away.

Where the Duo fights back is video and foldable-only tricks. It records 4K Dolby Vision at up to 120 fps on the main camera, and the folding design enables shots no slab phone can do: take a selfie with the higher-resolution rear cameras while previewing yourself on the outer display, or use Duo Preview so the person you are photographing can see themselves and adjust their pose. The 12MP Center Stage front camera on the outer display and the under-display FaceTime camera inside mean video calls work in any posture, propped up hands-free. For stills versatility — especially zoom — the Z Fold 7 wins. For video quality and creative shooting modes, the Duo is the more interesting camera.

4. Displays: Size, Brightness and the Crease in Daily Use

The inner screens are closer than the marketing suggests: 8.0 inches on the Z Fold 7 against 7.6 inches on the Duo (7.58 inches measured as a standard rectangle), both 120 Hz LTPO panels with adaptive refresh rates. Samsung's panel is 1968×2184 pixels; Apple's is 1878×2670 at 430 pixels per inch. In practice both are sharp enough that pixels disappear, and both are bright enough for outdoor use — Samsung rates its panels at 2,600 nits peak, while Apple quotes 1,000 nits typical, 1,600 nits for HDR and 3,000 nits peak outdoors. Treat those headline numbers with caution: the two companies measure peak brightness differently, so the figures are not directly comparable. What matters is that neither screen will wash out in sunlight.

The real display difference is the cover screen. Samsung's 6.5-inch, 21:9 cover display is a normal phone screen — you can live on it all day and only open the phone when you want the tablet. Apple's 5.4-inch outer display is deliberately small, about the size of a passport when closed, with roughly 90 percent of the screen area of an iPhone 18 Pro. Apple clearly expects Duo owners to open the phone for anything serious and use the cover for quick glances, calls and notifications. That makes the Duo the more pocketable device closed, and the Fold 7 the more capable device closed. If you often use your phone one-handed on a train or while carrying coffee, Samsung's cover screen is the practical winner; if you want the smallest possible folded phone and don't mind opening it constantly, the Duo's approach makes sense.

One more quiet advantage for the Duo: both of its displays share the same aspect ratio, so content scales proportionally as you move between them. And its nano-texture inner finish, borrowed from Apple's Pro Display playbook, cuts reflections in bright rooms in a way no other phone display does.

5. Performance, Battery Life and Charging

Both phones are overpowered for everyday use, and the differences only show up under sustained load. Apple's A20 Pro is built on a 2-nanometer process — a generation ahead of the Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy's 3-nanometer process — with a 6-core CPU, a 7-core GPU and a dual 16-core Neural Engine. Apple claims up to 35 percent better sustained performance than the iPhone 17 Pro, thanks in part to a custom vapor chamber and a chip package borrowed from its M-series Mac silicon. The Snapdragon 8 Elite for Galaxy pairs two Oryon cores running at 4.47 GHz with six efficiency cores and 12 GB of RAM (16 GB on the 1 TB model). For gaming, video editing and on-device AI, both are flagship-class; the A20 Pro's process advantage should show up mainly in efficiency and sustained performance rather than in anything you notice opening apps.

Battery is where the two makers stop speaking the same language. Samsung publishes a capacity: 4,400 mAh, with 25 W wired charging (50 percent in 30 minutes), 15 W wireless and 4.5 W reverse wireless. Apple publishes no milliamp-hour figure at all — the Duo has a dual-battery design, one cell on each side, and Apple rates it in usage hours instead: up to 31 hours of video playback on the inner display, up to 44 hours on the outer display, and up to 24 hours of mixed typical use. Fast charging is 50 percent in around 20 minutes wired, but only with a 60 W or higher adapter that is sold separately; wirelessly it is 50 percent in around 30 minutes with a MagSafe or Qi2 charger and a 35 W adapter. Because the metrics differ, no honest spec-sheet comparison can declare a battery winner — the Fold 7's 4,400 mAh is modest for a phone with an 8-inch screen, and the Duo's hours look strong on paper, but independent battery testing is what will settle it.

6. Durability, Water Resistance and Repair Costs

The Duo is the tougher phone on paper. Its IP68 rating covers immersion to 6 meters for 30 minutes, and the grade-5 titanium frame is wrapped in Ceramic Shield 2 on the front and Ceramic Shield on the back, which Apple says is three times more scratch-resistant than the previous generation. The Z Fold 7 is rated IP48: it survives immersion to 1.5 meters, but the "4" in its dust rating means protection only against solid objects larger than 1 mm — fine dust and beach sand can still reach the hinge. Both phones survive rain and splashes without drama; neither warranty covers water damage, so treat the ratings as insurance against accidents, not permission to swim with your phone.

The folding display itself is the durability question neither spec sheet answers. It is the most expensive component to replace on any foldable, which is why AppleCare+ is worth pricing into your budget before you buy. Cases help: Apple's Duo Case costs $79 and the Folio with kickstand $129, and both are MagSafe-compatible. Whichever foldable you choose, buy the case and the coverage on day one; a foldable without either is a $1,999 gamble.

7. Who Each Foldable Is For: Real-World Use Cases

The road warrior. If your phone is your office — email on one side, spreadsheet on the other, a document open while you take a call — the Z Fold 7 is the pick. Three-app split screen plus DeX means you can leave the laptop in the hotel on light travel days, and seven years of updates means the $1,999 stretches further.

The photographer and traveler. The Fold 7's 3x telephoto is the difference between a usable portrait at a wedding and a blurry digital crop. But if video is your medium, the Duo's 4K120 Dolby Vision recording and rear-camera selfies with an outer-display preview are genuinely new capabilities, not spec-sheet padding.

The student and note-taker. This is the Duo's category to lose — a 7.6-inch writing surface with Apple Pencil support and apps like Freeform is a compelling lecture companion. Two caveats: Pencil support arrived with iOS 27.2 on 26 October 2026, so the Duo needs that update installed, and the Fold 7 offers no stylus at all, so Samsung is not even in this race.

The reader and streamer. Either phone is excellent here, but the Fold 7's larger 8.0-inch inner display and wider aspect ratio make it the better mini-tablet for comics, PDFs and split-screen reading. The Duo's nano-texture finish is the better screen for bright rooms.

The one-handed commuter. The Fold 7's 6.5-inch cover display is a full phone in your hand; the Duo's 5.4-inch cover is smaller and more pocketable but demands you open the phone for real work. Choose based on how often you use your phone with one hand full.

The ecosystem loyalist. This is the deciding factor for most buyers. If your watch, laptop, earbuds and cloud storage are Apple's, the Duo slots into a continuity story — Handoff, AirDrop, Apple Watch unlock, iCloud — that Samsung cannot replicate. If your world is Android, Windows and Galaxy accessories, the Fold 7 with DeX and Link to Windows is the equivalent. Switching ecosystems to get the other foldable costs far more than the $1,999 sticker price suggests.

8. What We'd Buy and Why

Both phones start at $1,999, so this is not a price decision — it is an ecosystem and timing decision. If you live in Apple's world and want a foldable now, buy the Duo: it is the first folding iPhone, it has the better water resistance, the brighter outdoor display and the only stylus option, and its A20 Pro should age well. Go in with your eyes open: it is a first-generation product, and as a brand-new launch it is the harder of the two to find at a discount.

If you want the mature foldable, or the better value, buy the Galaxy Z Fold 7. It has been on sale since July 2025, so it is the one you will find discounted; it has the more versatile camera system, the deeper multitasking toolkit with DeX, and it is lighter in the pocket despite the bigger screen. Its weaknesses — the modest 4,400 mAh battery and the IP48 dust rating — are known quantities after a year of real-world use, not launch-day unknowns.

Our default recommendation: Apple households should buy the Duo; Android households should buy the Fold 7; and anyone without a strong ecosystem tie should buy the Fold 7 today, because a year on the market has made it the cheaper way into a flagship foldable. We have not lab-tested either phone — this recommendation rests on published manufacturer specifications and independent benchmark reporting, not hands-on testing — so treat it as a research-based starting point and check current street prices before you buy.

4. Which Foldable Ecosystem Fits Your Workflow?

If you depend on complex spreadsheet multitasking and desktop DeX, the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 7 remains the reigning champion. If you prioritize Apple ecosystem continuity — AirDrop, Apple Watch synergy, and ProMotion color calibration — the iPhone Duo brings the foldable form factor to iOS for the first time, starting at $1,999.

One final consideration is timing. The Z Fold 7 is a known quantity after more than a year on sale, with real-world durability and battery data behind it; the Duo is weeks old, and first-generation foldables historically improve fastest in their second year. If you upgrade phones every year, that hardly matters. If you keep a phone for three or four years, the Fold 7's seven-year update promise and the Duo's brand-new platform both argue for themselves — pick the ecosystem you will still be happy with in 2029.

Note: Samsung has since moved on — the Galaxy Z Fold8 and Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra were announced on 22 July 2026 and went on sale in August 2026, so the Z Fold 7 is no longer Samsung's newest foldable. This comparison keeps the Duo-versus-Z-Fold-7 pairing because both start at $1,999; if you want Samsung's latest hardware, compare the Duo against the Z Fold8 instead.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Which foldable supports a stylus?

Only the iPhone Duo: the table lists Apple Pencil USB-C support for the Duo, while the Galaxy Z Fold 7 has no stylus support at all. If handwritten notes or drawing on the big screen matter to you, that single row decides the comparison — Samsung's foldable is a viewing and multitasking device, not a writing one.

Which foldable has the better camera system?

For versatility, the Z Fold 7: a 200MP main sensor plus a 12MP ultra-wide and a 10MP 3x telephoto covers every focal length, while the Duo's 48MP Dual Fusion system (main plus ultra-wide) has no telephoto at all. For pure main-camera image quality, the Duo's larger Fusion sensor and Apple's processing close the gap — but zoom photography belongs to Samsung here.

How do water resistance ratings compare?

The Duo is rated IP68, up to 6 meters for 30 minutes, against the Z Fold 7's IP48, water resistant up to 1.5 meters. Both survive rain and splashes, but the Duo's deeper rating is the safer pick for poolside or beach use — with the usual caveat that no warranty covers water damage.

Which multitasking system is better?

It follows your workflow: One UI offers 3-app split screen plus floating windows and Samsung DeX for a desktop-like mode, while iOS 27 offers Split View, dual-window apps, and StandBy across displays. DeX is the difference-maker for laptop replacement; iOS 27's StandBy is the smoother second-screen experience for Apple households.

Do both foldables cost the same?

Yes, at the starting tier: both start from $1,999, the Duo at 256GB. The difference is timing — the Z Fold 7 has been on sale since July 2025, while the Duo took pre-orders October 16, 2026 and went on sale October 23, 2026 — so Samsung's foldable is the one you can find discounted first.

If you still cannot decide, choose by the phone you already own: the Z Fold 7 rewards Samsung households with DeX and the wider Android foldable app library, while the Duo rewards Apple households with iOS 27 continuity and Pencil support. The hardware gap is smaller than the ecosystem gap.
